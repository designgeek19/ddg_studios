1. Executive summary

Security is part of how DDGTech Studio designs and builds products from the first sprint, not a step added at the end. This whitepaper explains the principles we follow, the controls we apply across the delivery lifecycle, how we test and respond to problems, how we protect personal data, and how this website itself is secured.

It is written for clients, partners, regulators and technical reviewers who want to understand how we work. It describes our general approach, and the exact controls used on a project are agreed with the client in the project scope.

What this means for your project

  • We identify what needs protecting and who might attack it before we write code.
  • We build with secure defaults: least privilege, strong authentication, encryption and logging.
  • We test against the OWASP Top 10 and fix findings before launch.
  • We tell you promptly if something goes wrong, and we help you meet your legal duties.

2. Our security principles

These principles come from our core value of Security by Design, and they guide design and engineering decisions on every project.

Security by design

Threat modelling, secure patterns and security requirements are part of discovery and architecture, so the foundation is secure before features are added.

Least privilege

People, services and applications get only the access they need, for only as long as they need it, and access is reviewed regularly.

Defence in depth

No single control is trusted alone. We layer protections across identity, application, data, network and monitoring.

Zero trust

We verify identity and context for every request, and we do not trust a user or device just because it is inside a network.

Privacy by design

We collect the minimum personal data needed, protect it throughout its life and delete it when it is no longer required.

Transparency

We document decisions, share findings plainly, and keep clients informed through the whole project and after launch.

5. Application security controls

Identity and access

  • Strong password hashing, multi-factor authentication for administrative and sensitive accounts, and secure session handling.
  • Role-based access control with least privilege, checked on the server side for every sensitive action.

Data protection

  • Encryption in transit with modern TLS, and encryption at rest for sensitive data.
  • Sensitive fields masked or tokenised where possible, and logs kept free of secrets and unnecessary personal data.

Input, APIs and logic

  • Validation of input, safe handling of output and parameterised queries to reduce injection and cross-site scripting risks.
  • API authentication, rate limiting and schema validation, and protection against common abuse such as brute-force attempts.

Dependencies and secrets

  • Dependencies are kept to what is needed, pinned and reviewed, and we check for known vulnerabilities.
  • Secrets and keys are stored in a secrets manager or environment configuration, never in source code.

Logging and monitoring

  • Security-relevant events such as sign-ins, permission changes and failures are logged with timestamps, with alerts for suspicious activity.

Mobile apps

  • Sensitive data stored in the platform's secure storage, biometric login where appropriate, and safe handling of offline data and retries when connections drop.

6. Cloud and infrastructure security

  • Identity and access management with least-privilege roles, separate environments for development, staging and production, and no shared accounts.
  • Network controls and segmentation, so that databases and internal services are not exposed to the public internet unless they must be.
  • Automated, encrypted backups with tested restoration, and clear recovery targets agreed with the client.
  • Regular patching and configuration review, and infrastructure defined in code where practical so changes can be reviewed.
  • Logging, alerting and cost and usage monitoring, with a clear owner for each alert.

7. Security testing and assessments

We test our own work and, when engaged, assess our clients' systems. We only test systems that the client owns or is authorised to test, under a written scope and rules of engagement.

Our testers use tools including Nmap, OWASP ZAP, Burp Suite and Wireshark on Linux, together with manual review. Findings are rated by severity, for example using CVSS, and the most serious are fixed first. We re-test every fix before we call it closed.

8. Data protection and privacy

  • Minimise. We collect only the personal data a feature needs, and we avoid sensitive data unless it is essential.
  • Protect. Personal data is encrypted, access is restricted and logged, and production data is not used in development without safeguards.
  • Retain. We agree retention periods with clients and delete or anonymise data when they end.
  • Respect the law. We design for the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and, where it applies, the GDPR, including support for access, correction and deletion requests.
  • Transfers and suppliers. We choose providers carefully, record where data is processed, and use appropriate safeguards for cross-border transfers.
  • Breaches. If a personal data breach is likely to put people at risk, we help clients meet their duty to notify the regulator within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, and to inform affected people where required.

Details of how we handle personal data on this website are in our Privacy Policy.

9. How this website is secured

This website is a static site with no database or user accounts, which keeps its attack surface small. These protections are in place:

  • Served over HTTPS only, with HTTP Strict Transport Security.
  • A Content Security Policy that limits where scripts, styles, images, fonts and frames can load from, plus frame protection (X-Frame-Options and frame-ancestors).
  • Other security headers: X-Content-Type-Options, Referrer-Policy, Permissions-Policy and Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy.
  • Forms are handled by our hosting provider's form service, with a hidden spam-trap field and checks before sending. No payment details are collected.
  • No advertising or analytics cookies. The only item stored in your browser is your light or dark theme choice.
  • Third-party content is kept to a minimum. The booking calendar loads only if you choose to open it.
  • The site's code is kept in version control, and changes are deployed through an automated build from the repository.

10. Our people and internal practices

  • Unique accounts for every team member, multi-factor authentication wherever the service supports it, and access removed promptly when someone leaves a project or the team.
  • Team members use a password manager, keep devices updated and use screen locks and disk encryption.
  • Client data and credentials are shared only through approved, secure channels, and are not sent by plain email or chat where avoidable.
  • Security awareness is part of onboarding and is refreshed regularly, including how to spot phishing and social engineering.
  • Team members are bound by confidentiality, and clients can request a non-disclosure agreement before sharing details.
  • Our cybersecurity lead reviews high-risk designs and releases.

11. Incident response

When we suspect a security incident affecting us or a client, we follow a defined process:

PhaseWhat we do
PrepareKeep contact lists, runbooks, logging and backups ready, so a response does not start from nothing.
Detect and triageConfirm the event, assess severity and impact, and assign a lead.
ContainStop the spread, for example by isolating systems or revoking credentials, while preserving evidence.
Eradicate and recoverRemove the cause, fix the weakness, restore from clean backups and watch for recurrence.
NotifyInform the affected client without undue delay, and support notifications to regulators and affected people where the law requires.
LearnHold a blameless review and record the lessons and the fixes that follow.

12. Vulnerability disclosure

We welcome reports from security researchers and anyone who finds a weakness in our website or services. Please report responsibly.

How to report

  • Email dataanddesigngeeks@gmail.com with the subject “Security report”.
  • Include the affected address, clear steps to reproduce the issue, what you expected and what happened, and any proof of concept.
  • Give us a reasonable time to fix the issue before you share it publicly.

What we will do

  • Acknowledge your report within three (3) business days.
  • Assess it, keep you updated, and fix confirmed issues as quickly as we can.
  • Credit you, if you wish, once the issue is resolved.

Good-faith research

We will not take legal action against people who follow this policy: act in good faith, avoid privacy violations and disruption, do not access or change data that is not yours, and stop and tell us when you reach sensitive data. Out of scope: denial-of-service attacks, social engineering of our team or clients, physical attacks, spam, and findings that only affect outdated or unsupported browsers.

Our machine-readable contact details are at /.well-known/security.txt.
